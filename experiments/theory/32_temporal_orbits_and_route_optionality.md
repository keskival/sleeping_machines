# Delay alternatives form a temporal control orbit

Derived 30 September 2026. E143 improves private SHD accuracy from 370/512 to
408/512; E145 resets its learned hidden clocks to initialization and gets
395/512, without changing source vectors, state/value maps or its readout.
This shows dependence of this fitted classifier on learned timing. It does
not prove a matched fixed-clock learner could not learn comparable accuracy.

## 234. Exact clock alternatives inside a stable ordering cell

For a constant linear state generator A and event drive b_i at time t_i,
the drive's contribution to a later state at fixed time T is
h_i(T)=exp(A(T-ti)) b_i. Moving only this event by delta, without crossing
another event or changing its drive, changes that later state by exactly

\[
 d_i(\delta;T)=(e^{-A\delta}-I)h_i(T). \tag{234.1}
\]

At the moved event itself, the old-history state is instead transported by
exp(A delta), while the newly injected b_i stays unaged. Its state changes by
(exp(A delta)-I)(s_i-b_i). These are different local counterfactuals.

The adjacent-interval teacher makes this distinction precise. Write
s_i=F_i s_{i-1}+b_i, F_i=exp(A(ti-ti-1)); let v_i be the complete adjoint of
s_i and q_i its direct readout adjoint, so v_i=q_i+F_{i+1}^T v_{i+1}. Timing
ti occurs in both neighboring intervals, giving

\[
 g_{t_i}=\langle v_i,A F_i s_{i-1}\rangle
       -\langle v_{i+1},A F_{i+1}s_i\rangle
 =\langle q_i,A(s_i-b_i)\rangle
       -\langle v_{i+1},A F_{i+1}b_i\rangle. \tag{234.2}
\]

The contribution of older history cancels between the two adjacent suffix
terms by the semigroup law. Timing teaches both how the current readout
samples old history and how this event is placed relative to later evidence.
For the last event, there is no following interval; its query-time dependence
must be stated separately. Input-/state-dependent coefficients, time-dependent
drives, count normalization and nonlinear suffixes add their own terms.

For one linear state followed by a fixed linear class map C at T, the
counterfactual logit displacement is exactly C d_i(delta;T). Its finite
cross-entropy difference can then be evaluated without a whole route tree.
Nonlinear later emissions need a suffix replay or a declared approximation.
Equation (234.1) alone is not exact full-network race credit across changed
orders or altered nonlinear descendants.

## 235. Many near-tied clocks can still offer almost one usable direction

The clock orbit expands as

\[
 d(\delta)=\sum_{j\geq1}(-\delta)^j A^j h/j!. \tag{235.1}
\]

By Cayley–Hamilton, its span lies in the Krylov space generated by
Ah,A^2h,...,A^d h. Conversely, on any open delay interval, its derivatives at
zero generate those vectors, so the orbit span equals that Krylov space.
Distinct damped oscillatory modes with nonzero drive components can have full
real-pair rank. A fixed vector plus multiple delays can therefore generate
many finite alternative states; it is not inherently a one-dimensional linear
representation. At first order, however, every small clock alternative is
parallel to Ah.

For K alternatives delta_k=epsilon c_k, form D=[d(delta_1),...,d(delta_K)].
If the first r Krylov columns are independent, K=r and the distinct c_k are
nonzero, the projected determinant has leading magnitude

\[
 |\det D_r|=\epsilon^{r(r+1)/2}
 \left|\det[Ah,\ldots,A^r h]\right|
 \frac{|\det[c_k^j]_{j,k=1}^r|}{\prod_{j=1}^r j!}
 +o(\epsilon^{r(r+1)/2}), \tag{235.2}
\]

where the Krylov determinant is expressed in a fixed orthonormal basis of
its r-dimensional span. Under these nondegeneracy assumptions its ordered
singular scales are generically epsilon,epsilon^2,...,epsilon^r. Higher
directions exist algebraically but become unusably small near a tie. Duplicate
delays or missing modal drive components reduce rank further.

Here "fixed vector" holds one observed drive constant while varying its delay
to isolate the timing control orbit. It is not a restriction on the payload of
the learned encoder. `EventStateBlock` projects the incoming vector into modal
memory, mixes a learned memory read with the direct input, gates that mixture,
and emits a residual update to the incoming vector. Both content and timing
therefore carry information; the orbit calculation studies one axis at a time.

This resolves a concrete counting ambiguity: K clock choices per layer do not
give K independent infinitesimal control directions. Finite temporal spread,
distinct modes/source vectors, and their downstream observation all matter.
With a bounded physical work/update budget, rank without conditioning is not
enough. Near-tie availability can help race teaching while an excessively
narrow delay pool suppresses richer temporal reserve. “Edge of chaos” alone
does not specify a useful initialization.

## 236. Use the orbit to measure optionality, not raw uncertainty

At a fixed differentiable suffix, let H map local state perturbations to
class contrasts. A cost-weighted observable route Gramian is

\[
 W=H D\,\operatorname{diag}(w_k)D^T H^T,\quad w_k\geq0. \tag{236.1}
\]

Clock alternatives only enlarge task-visible reserve when their orbit chords
survive H and remain attainable under the delay/work budget. New payloads,
addresses or parallel active routes can add different h/A programs and enlarge
the union of Krylov spaces, rather than merely adding nearly duplicate clocks.
Depth adds useful controls only when the suffix keeps their observed directions
distinct. This is a concrete actuator geometry for §§171–172 and §212.

W is a local control-support diagnostic, not the expected value of subsequent
learning. Its trace does not retain orientation or guarantee a useful future
correction. A scalar continuation-value backup can still represent a declared
horizon/work-conditioned optionality value (§152), but its targets must observe
future adaptation outcomes. Replacing that value by route entropy, variance
or immediate class loss would change its meaning. Retaining the geometry is
particularly useful for deciding which sparse counterfactuals to propose.

The next clock/payload initialization experiment should therefore measure
observable orbit singular scales and finite suffix utility, then spend its
counterfactual budget on distinct reachable programs. E143's success does not
license a seed sweep or unrestricted random delay noise. Its modal-parameter
reset changes only one held decision (408 to 407); source/state-stack/clock
resets have much larger effects. Those are fitted-coordinate reset diagnostics,
not additive component improvements or a matched training comparison.

**Completed E148 numerical contract:** exact own/later-state clock
counterfactual errors are at most 1.59e-15; the adjacent-interval teacher error
is 2.06e-13. Four independent orbit directions have measured small-delay
singular exponents 0.99990,1.99993,3.00005,4.00004. At epsilon=0.001 their
condition number is 1.33e9. This verifies the proposed geometry in a linear,
fixed-order example, not a trained optionality objective or nonlinear suffix.
