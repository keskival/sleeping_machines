## Theory: why exposure alone need not fix learning

For ONE episode, condition on entering history H, actual first time T and
independent future draws E. Do not condition on the whole current
candidate-exponential vector, since it reveals W. At the sampled site,
let C_W be the native pathwise content/time gradient and A the detached-
return categorical gradient, invariant to that site's current winner.
G_f=C_W+R*A and G_j=sum pi_i*C_i+R*A. Then

    E[G_f | H,T,E] = G_j,
    trace Var(G_f | H,T,E) = sum pi_i*||C_i-G_j+R*A||^2,
    Var(G_j | H,T,E) = 0.

The remaining entering-history, first-time, future and sampled-site noise
is not removed. Shared candidate draws couple episode winners, so summing
these separate conditional averages is NOT generally the joint conditional
expectation of the full batch estimator. Cross-episode covariance can
change adversely. The theorem is about declared native pathwise gradients,
not exact derivatives of every future hard boundary or whole expected risk.
Clipping/Adam are nonlinear; mean preservation before clipping does not
preserve expected updates. Thus correct losing gradients can coexist with
worse finite predictions and more computation without contradiction.

Even an actual total variance reduction would need to repay1.707x fitting
cost. A heuristic variance-times-cost screen is useful only under declared
optimization assumptions, not a convergence/Adam theorem. Do not assume
extra counterfactuals or content exposure are automatically advantageous.
The current negative fit does not prove that sparse temporal computation
lacks representational capacity, or that branch averaging can never help.
It rejects promotion from this particular admission.
