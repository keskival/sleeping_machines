# Investor-reading review — 3 October 2026

The first deck documented the research well but made an investor do too much
translation. It had 22 main slides, used internal configuration names in key
charts, described several markets before choosing a first product, and gave
speculative exit arithmetic a prominent role in the valuation argument.

The revised deck has **16 main slides and 16 optional diligence slides**. The
main pitch answers, in order: what economic problem we address, how the
architecture approaches it, what the experiments establish, what we would
sell first, why a hardware ecosystem might care, who executes the work, what
can fail, and what €3M is intended to establish.

Concrete editorial changes:

- Replaced internal names such as p32/d4 with “small model,” “more memory,”
  “deeper model,” “medium model” and “best quality.” An appendix defines their
  widths, layers and memory slots and maps them to reproducible records.
- Explained bits per character as prediction error before showing the chart;
  kept lower-is-better directions and clearly distinguished training arithmetic
  from service cost. Presented near-recurrent quality alongside its extra work.
- Removed conversational corrections such as “LUMI is not a model.” The AMD
  account now directly states the relevant positive precedent and its scope.
- Chose a **product hypothesis**: model and runtime for low-batch language
  inference on existing hardware. This is an evaluation target, not validated
  demand or a demonstrated region of commercial advantage.
- Replaced main-deck billion-euro spend and hundred-million-device examples
  with annual economics per prospective customer: €1M eligible cost, €200k
  assumed gross saving, €40k assumed license, €160k buyer benefit before
  transition costs. Twenty-five such deployments imply €1M annual revenue.
  All are explicit assumptions; no revenue, interest or willingness to pay exists.
- Connected the budget and hiring to three proof gates: scale/replication,
  complete execution economics, and external evaluation/adoption.
- Kept the €50M proposed pre-money and its 5.66% initial investor stake in the
  financing slide. Moved conditional €10B/€50B exits, the €100M stretch and
  reverse-calculated success odds into the appendix. Those calculations do
  not prove a valuation. The small ownership stake may limit investor fit;
  changing the story does not resolve price or financing-market uncertainty.
- Preserved the detailed credit, capacity, sparse execution, online-learning,
  negative-result and protocol disclosures in the appendix and notes. Added
  PDF bookmarks for a short main-pitch reading path.

This is an editorial revision using the **same frozen completed evidence**.
It makes no new research or measured economic claim. The ambition remains a
model/runtime/temporal computing substrate across cloud and embodied systems.
Commercial application quality, measured energy/traffic, trained sparse parity,
longer-scale evidence, retention, exclusive rights and customer adoption remain
the substantive questions investors must evaluate.

Remaining diligence cannot be fixed by wording: founder availability, corporate
entity and cap table, contribution/employer IP rights, intended licenses, actual
compute and hiring quotes, modern replicated controls, service measurements,
and prospective evaluation partners. Earlier public disclosures remain relevant
despite the repository now being private. This work makes no external contact
and does not publish the deck.
